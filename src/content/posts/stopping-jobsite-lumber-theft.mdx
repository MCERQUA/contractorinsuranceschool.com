Ask any framing contractor what their most common insurance claim is, and you'll hear the same answer over and over: **"They took my lumber."** Or the nailers. Or the generator. Jobsite theft is the single most frequent loss framers suffer — and it's also one of the most preventable and the most claimable, if you set it up right.

## Why framing jobsites are a target

A fresh lumber package, neatly banded and staged on a slab, is essentially cash sitting in the open. So are nailers, miter saws, and compressors left on site overnight. Framing jobsites are usually unsecured, in early-stage neighborhoods with little traffic, and loaded with exactly the commodities thieves can resell fast. That combination makes them a magnet.

## Prevention: cheap, effective, and the best insurance there is

Insurance pays after a loss; prevention means there's no loss to pay. The high-leverage moves:

- **Don't stage more than you need.** Coordinate delivery so materials arrive close to when they're installed.
- **Secure the package.** Chain-of-custody matters: photograph the staged package, lock what you can, and consider a jobsite box for small tools.
- **Lighting and visibility.** Motion lights and a cleared sightline discourage opportunistic theft.
- **Serial numbers and engraving.** Mark tools and record serial numbers. It helps both recovery and the claim.
- **Take tools home.** The simplest: don't leave nailers and saws on the site overnight if you can avoid it.

## Make sure the right coverage is in place

When theft happens despite prevention, the question is whether you have the right policy. As we cover in [our builder's risk vs. tools floater guide](/blog/builders-risk-vs-tools-floater), the two theft losses map to two policies:

- **Stolen lumber or materials** → builder's risk or an installation floater.
- **Stolen tools or equipment** → a tools & equipment (inland marine) floater.

If you carry neither, a theft is entirely out of pocket. If you carry both, scheduled at replacement cost, a theft is a phone call and a check.

## Documenting a theft claim

When you discover the loss, the speed and quality of your documentation decides whether the claim pays cleanly. Do this:

1. **File a police report immediately.** Most carriers require it for theft claims, and it starts a paper trail.
2. **Photograph everything** — the scene, what's missing, any damage.
3. **Provide purchase records and serial numbers** for tools, and delivery tickets for materials.
4. **Notify your broker the same day.** We help you file correctly and push it forward.

Good documentation turns a "maybe" claim into a paid one.

## The margin math

A stolen lumber package can be $5,000–$15,000 or more. A full set of nailers, saws, and a compressor is another $2,000–$5,000. Uninsured, that comes straight out of the job's profit — and sometimes out of your own pocket. Insured and documented, you're back on schedule with a check in hand.
