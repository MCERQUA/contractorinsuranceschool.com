# How Much Does Glulam Manufacturer Insurance Cost?

If you run a glue-laminated timber plant, you already know your insurance isn't priced like a generic manufacturer's. A single structural beam you produce can end up holding up a school, an arena, or an office building — and that product-liability exposure, combined with combustible-dust property risk and expensive press and kiln equipment, puts you in a specialty underwriting class all your own.

So what does it actually cost? The honest answer is that no two glulam plants get the same number, but the factors that drive your premium are consistent. Here's what moves the needle.

## The coverages that make up your premium

A real glulam manufacturer program isn't one policy — it's a coordinated set of lines, each priced separately:

- **Product liability** — usually the largest single cost for a structural manufacturer, because the claim severity on a beam that fails in service can be enormous.
- **Commercial property** — your plant, kilns, presses, lumber yard, and finished-goods inventory, rated for combustible-dust and hot-work exposure.
- **Boiler & machinery (equipment breakdown)** — covers the presses, kilns, CNC lines, and boilers your property policy won't.
- **General liability** — premises and operations, including products-completed operations.
- **Workers' compensation** — class-coded for sawmill, planing, pressing, and finishing operations.
- **Commercial auto** — flatbeds and step-decks hauling oversized beams.
- **Inland marine / stock throughput** — mobile equipment and goods in transit.
- **Umbrella / excess liability** — layered limits above the primary program.

## What drives the number

Underwriters look at a handful of variables to price each line:

1. **Products and end use.** Architectural glulam going into public or commercial structures carries more product-liability exposure than commodity dimensional product. Curved members and custom-engineered beams add complexity.
2. **Annual revenue and units shipped.** Product liability is usually rated against sales volume — more product in the field means more potential claim exposure.
3. **Plant and equipment values.** Property and equipment-breakdown limits follow the replacement cost of your facility, kilns, hydraulic and RF curing presses, CNC lines, and boilers.
4. **Payroll and crew size by operation.** Workers' comp is rated on payroll, assigned to class codes that reflect the actual machinery each crew runs.
5. **Fleet and transit exposure.** Oversize-load hauling, delivery radius, and the value of finished beams in transit all affect auto and inland marine.
6. **Loss history.** Five years of loss runs are standard. A dust fire, a delamination claim, or an OSHA citation will all affect pricing — but they don't automatically make you uninsurable.
7. **Quality documentation.** Plants that can show ANSI A190.1 conformance, ICC-ES evaluation reports, lot tracking, and a documented housekeeping and hot-work program get better numbers because the underwriter can see the risk is controlled.

## Rough ranges (and why they're only a starting point)

We won't pretend to quote your plant in a blog post, but to set expectations: a smaller commodity producer with a single line might invest a few thousand to low-five-figures a year across the core lines, while a multi-shift architectural glulam plant with significant product liability and high property values can run well into six figures for a full coordinated program. The spread is wide because the exposures are wide.

The single biggest lever on price isn't haggling — it's **how your risk is presented to underwriters**. A plant that's been declined by a standard carrier often gets a better number from a specialty timber market once its housekeeping, machine-guarding, and QC documentation are packaged properly.

## How to get the best number

- **Bundle the program with one specialist.** Coordinating product liability, property, equipment breakdown, and auto under one program is usually cheaper — and far less gap-prone — than buying each line from a different source.
- **Document your risk controls.** NFPA 664 housekeeping, hot-work permits, machine guarding per OSHA 1910.263, and ANSI A190.1 QC all reduce what you pay.
- **Schedule equipment at real replacement cost.** Underinsured presses and kilns cost you at claim time; accurately valued schedules sometimes improve property terms.
- **Shop specialty markets.** Generic manufacturer carriers misprice glulam. Specialty timber markets price it correctly — and we have those relationships.
