General liability is the policy every general contractor requires you to carry before you set the first plate. But not every GL policy actually covers the work you do — and the most dangerous gap for a framing contractor is the one you'll only discover after a fall: the **height exclusion**.

## Framing is a height business

Framers work on top plates, scaffolding, and roofs. Two-story and three-story framing, truss setting, and roof work are core operations — not edge cases. So a GL policy that **excludes work over one or two stories**, or that excludes roof work entirely, doesn't cover the bulk of what your crew actually does.

## How the exclusion hides

It usually isn't loud. It's a clause buried in the policy's exclusions or endorsements — "no coverage for operations exceeding two stories in height," or a roof-work exclusion, or a limitation tied to "open-frame construction." On a one-page quote summary you'll never see it. In the policy jacket, it's the difference between a paid claim and a denied one.

We see it most often in the cheapest policies — the $39/month "contractor coverage" advertised online. The low price exists *because* the carrier excluded the riskiest (and most common) framing work.

## The fall claim that isn't covered

Here's how it plays out: a framer slips on a top plate at 18 feet, is injured (or worse), and a claim is filed. The carrier's adjuster opens the policy, reads the height exclusion, and denies coverage — because the work was above the excluded height. Now the medical bills, the lost wages, the third-party liability, and the defense costs are on *you*. A "covered" operation turns into a business-ending loss.

The same dynamic applies to **roof work** and **truss setting** exclusions. If your policy excludes them and you do them, you're uninsured for exactly the claims you're most likely to have.

## What to buy instead

A framing GL policy should **explicitly cover**:

- Two- and three-story framing
- Roof framing and sheathing
- Truss setting
- Work from scaffolding and lifts
- Subcontracted operations (with certificate tracking)

And it should come from a carrier that actually writes framing contractors — not a generic small-business market that prices the policy assuming you'll never leave the ground.

## How to check your current policy

1. **Read the exclusions section** of your policy jacket, not just the declarations page.
2. **Search for "height," "stories," "roof," and "open-frame."**
3. **Check whether truss setting is covered** if you do it.
4. **Ask your broker directly:** "Does this policy cover my crew framing at two and three stories and setting roof trusses?" Get the answer in writing.

If the answer is no — or "I'm not sure" — it's time to replace the policy before, not after, a fall.
